# Books Reading Order — AI Engineering

Recommended sequence. Do not try to read everything at once.

---

## Tier 1 — Start Here (Next 2–3 months)

| Priority | Book | Why | When |
|----------|------|-----|------|
| 1 | **AI Engineering** — Chip Huyen (O’Reilly, 2025) | Best single book on building with foundation models. Evaluation, RAG, agents, production thinking. | Start Week 1–2 |
| 2 | **Hands-On Large Language Models** — Jay Alammar & Maarten Grootendorst | Understand what is happening inside the models + practical code. | After or parallel with Chip Huyen |
| 3 | **LLM Engineer’s Handbook** — Paul Iusztin & Maxime Labonne | End-to-end production focus. Very practical. | After first MVP |

---

## Tier 2 — Deep Foundations

| Book | Why | When |
|------|-----|------|
| **Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch)** — Sebastian Raschka | True understanding by building a GPT-style model. | When you want deeper intuition (can be weekend project) |
| **Designing Machine Learning Systems** — Chip Huyen | Systems thinking that still applies heavily to AI Engineering. | Anytime you feel fuzzy on production ML/AI systems |

---

## Tier 3 — Specialized (Later)

- Prompt Engineering focused titles (O’Reilly)
- Advanced RAG / Enterprise RAG books (as we reach Module 2.6)
- Agent-specific books (when we enter Multi-Agent Systems)
- Fine-tuning / RLHF focused material (during Fine-Tuning track)

---

## Reading Rules

1. One main book at a time.
2. Take notes in the research block (11:30 PM).
3. Turn key insights into LinkedIn posts.
4. Code along whenever the book has exercises or companion repos.
5. Re-read chapters later when you hit that topic in the curriculum.
